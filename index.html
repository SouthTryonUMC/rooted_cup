01 DryingWhat rooted is

rooted is a neighborhood enterprise in South Charlotte. We roast coffee, we hire and train neighbors to do the work, and the people who live here help run it.

Brookhill Village and Southside are two public housing communities off South Tryon Street. The land around them has gotten expensive fast. Families who have been here for decades are watching the neighborhood change and doing the math on how long they get to stay.

rooted is one answer to that. A business that hires on these blocks, teaches a real trade, and keeps its money moving through the neighborhood instead of out of it. Most of the people we hire live in Brookhill Village or Southside. The wages land where they live.

02 First crackHow we work

What’s strong,
not wrong

Most funding conversations open with what a place is missing. We open with who is already here and what they already know how to do.

There is real capacity on these blocks. It doesn’t show up in a needs assessment and it rarely gets asked about. So we go looking for it, we name it out loud, and we build the business around the people we find.

Neighbors help author this work. Some on payroll, some at the table where decisions get made. That involvement is how the business is designed to run, not something we added on top of it.

03 DevelopmentThe enterprise

Coffee, roasted here

We’re starting with small-batch roasting. Coffee makes sense as a first business for a few plain reasons. You can begin without much capital. Roasting is a real skill somebody can learn and carry with them. The product keeps on a shelf for weeks. And churches, schools, and offices across Charlotte already buy a lot of it, week after week.

The people who do the roasting are hired as roasters, and roaster is the job title. Many of the people we hire are coming home from incarceration and picking up a craft they can take anywhere. We pay a wage a person can live on while they learn the trade, hourly, from the first day of training.

01

Anchor accounts

Standing wholesale orders from churches, offices, and partner organizations. Contracted, invoiced, and predictable enough to plan payroll around.

02

Subscriptions

Recurring bags shipped to households. Better margin, and it builds a base of people with a stake in how this turns out.

03

Pop-ups

Markets, events, and being present in the neighborhood. It doesn’t move much volume, but it’s how people meet us and how new hires learn the counter.

Steady accounts first. Payroll has to be reliable before anything else can be.

04 DropWhere this goes

The longer
plan

Coffee is the start. What we’re really building is the ability to run something well: hiring, training, pricing, keeping books, making decisions with the neighborhood rather than about it. Once we can do that, we can do it again with a second venture.

Community involvement stays at the center of it. Neighbors on staff, neighbors in leadership, and a business whose wages and decisions land here. The legal shape of that is still being decided, and we would rather leave it open than answer it badly. What is settled is that the people who live here help run it.

The vision

A world where everyone can belong, heal, grow, and flourish together.

Where we come from

rooted grew out of South Tryon Community United Methodist Church and the neighbors of Brookhill Village and Southside.

Who we learn from

Homeboy Industries in Los Angeles. Deep Time at Trinity UMC in Asheville, running a coffee social enterprise since 2023.

Where we are now

Active development. Equipment specified and the compliance pathway mapped. We’re raising toward launch.
