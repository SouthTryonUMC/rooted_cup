rooted. brand kit · v1.0 · August 2026 · direction: Cup
rooted.

Good work. Good coffee. Good news.

rooted is a neighborhood enterprise in South Charlotte. We roast coffee, we hire and train neighbors to do the work, and the people who live here help run it. Most of the people we hire have been through the justice system. We hire them as roasters, and roaster is the job title.

That paragraph is the brand in one place. The last two sentences are the fixed wording for naming the justice system: same words on the bag, the site, and the grant packet. Do not paraphrase them.

Type

Fraunces for display (Undercase Type). Instrument Sans for body (Rodrigo Fuenzalida). Courier Prime for labels and the roast log (Quote-Unquote Apps). All three are on Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, which covers web embedding and commercial print on packaging. Nothing to buy. For print, download the files from fonts.google.com and hand them to the printer with the license text.

Fraunces has four axes. The rule: optical size follows the point size, softness is always 100, and wonk is on in the wordmark only. Headings are upright and quiet. The wordmark is the one place the type gets to have a personality.

Wordmark and logo suite

Lowercase, with a period. The period is green in the primary and dark versions and matches the text in single-color use. Clear space on every side is the height of the o. The taproot

A vertical rule with nodes, used as structure. Each node marks a real step, in the order it happened. On the bag it is the roast log. On the site it is the left rule of a section that lists steps: the revenue order, the twelve months, the roast. It is never decoration, never a background pattern, and it never branches.

Anchor accounts

Standing wholesale orders from churches, schools, and offices. Contracted, invoiced, and predictable enough to plan payroll around.

Subscriptions

Recurring bags shipped to households. Better margin, and it builds a base of people with a stake in how this turns out.

Pop-ups

Markets, events, and being present in the neighborhood. It doesn't move much volume, but it's how people meet us and how new hires learn the counter.

The same device on the site, carrying the revenue order from the current index copy. The order is the information; the nodes just make it visible.

Spacing, grid, corners

Spacing

4px base. Steps: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128. Section padding 64 desktop, 48 mobile. Paragraph gap 16. Nothing lands between steps.

Grid

12 columns to 1120px, 24px gutter, 24px page margin. 4 columns on mobile, 16px gutter, 20px margin. Body text never wider than 62 characters.

Corners and borders

If it is not a node, it is not round. Every corner is square: buttons, fields, cards, photographs, tables. Borders are 1px hairlines in the rule color; emphasis borders are 1px in the text color. No drop shadows. Depth is a change of surface or a hairline.

Front

Wordmark top left. Origin line in Fraunces italic. Taproot with the four-entry log. Weight bottom left, tagline bottom right. Nothing else on the front. No percentage badge, no mission line, no photograph.

Back

The stacked lockup at the top. Then, in body type at 9pt or larger, the paragraph from the top of this page, word for word, including the two fixed sentences about the justice system. Then the transparency line: the importer's published farm price for this lot, in the log face. Then the church line: "rooted grew out of South Tryon Community United Methodist Church and the neighbors of Brookhill Village and Southside." Then the address and the legal name once the legal structure is decided. Leave a blank for it until then; do not print a placeholder name.

Spine

The spine lockup, Paper on Roast, with the per-lot label on the right.

Material

Unbleached or natural-white kraft with a matte finish, not glossy, not metallic. One-color print (Roast) plus green as a spot color if the printer can hold it; if not, use the single-color mark and let the green go. Never stretch the wordmark to fit a bag width. Scale it and leave the space.

Photography

Photograph a workplace. Hands mid-task. Green coffee in the bag it arrived in. The drum, the chaff tray, the cooling bin, a scoop of beans on the cupping table. The church building from the sidewalk on South Tryon. The room with the roaster in it, and the room with the tables in it, which is the same room.

Light

Daylight, from the side or behind. Morning on South Tryon if you can get it. No flash, no ring light, no studio backdrop. Let the room be the room.

Color

Warm neutral. No heavy filters, no fade, no orange-and-teal grade. The photograph should sit next to Paper and Roast without fighting them. If in doubt, less saturation, not more.

Framing

Close and level. The camera is at the height of the work, not above it. Square crops are fine; square corners always.

People

A cohort member is photographed the way a skilled worker is photographed: at work, competent, mid-task, not looking at the camera for sympathy. Faces only with written consent that the person can revoke at any time, and revocation means the photograph comes down everywhere within a week. Never before-and-after. Never a group photo that reads as staff versus program. If the pastor is in the frame, he is holding a scoop or a mug like everyone else.

Never

Clasped hands. A single tear. A person looking hopefully out a window. Anyone photographed to look poor. Stock photography of any kind. Photographs of Brookhill or Southside residences without the residents.

Voice

Short sentences. Concrete nouns. "We," never "the organization." "Neighbors," never "beneficiaries." Explain the business by how the money moves and who does the work, not by who it helps. Read every line out loud. If it sounds like a brochure, cut it. No epigram closing a paragraph. No three-item lists for rhythm. Go easy on em-dashes. Two registers at once: a workforce funder and a Sunday School class read the same sentence and both find it addressed to them.

Fixed lines. Use as written.

  • Naming the justice system: Most of the people we hire have been through the justice system. We hire them as roasters, and roaster is the job title.
  • Involvement: We roast coffee, we hire and train neighbors to do the work, and the people who live here help run it.
  • The church: rooted grew out of South Tryon Community United Methodist Church and the neighbors of Brookhill Village and Southside.
  • The honest number: Year one, the coffee pays part of the wages. Not all of them.
  • Sourcing: We buy from importers who publish what the farmer was paid, and we print it on the bag.
  • Tagline: Good work. Good coffee. Good news.
  • Sign-off: Let's grow something beautiful together. Stay rooted.
